We changed the main idea. Sonae now plans what olive mills do with their waste, so it feeds olive trees instead of polluting wadis, and proves it on a blockchain.

1. The idea in one minute

Every winter, each maâsra (olive mill, معصرة) presses olives into oil and is left with two wastes:

  • Margine (مرجين): dark, bitter water. Dumped in a wadi it kills plants, fish and wells. Spread thinly on an olive grove, at the right time, it is free water and potassium fertiliser.
  • Grignon (فيتورة): the dry paste of skins and pits. It burns like firewood, so hammams, bakeries and brick kilns can use it as fuel.

Sonae is the daily planner that sends each tanker of margine to the right grove on the right day, sends grignon to whoever needs fuel, and records every load on a public ledger nobody can edit.

Why it's different: pump schedulers and water filters are everywhere at this hackathon. Olive-mill waste is a Tunisian problem: it happens right now, November to February, and it ties water, energy and farming together in one service.

2. The core service: one decision a day

Every day, for each mill, Sonae looks at the weather forecast, the ponds and the groves, and decides:

Where

Only approved groves: not on a slope (over 8%), not within 100 m of a drinking-water well, not within 50 m of the wadi. Nearest grove first.

When

Never on a rainy day, the day before rain, or while the soil is still wet. Rain would wash margine into the wadis.

How much

At most 50 m³ per hectare per season and 10 m³ per hectare per day. The fullest pond is served first.

Then it sends a Derja SMS to the farmer and the tanker driver, for example:

Sonae: ghodwa 60 m3 margine men Maasra Ben Salah lel jnen mta3ek (4 kamyoun). Ma tsa9ich 3 ayem. Potassium bla flous lel zitoun.

Demo story (the Plan page): three mills near Sfax are pressing. A storm is coming on Wednesday. Sonae sends out 640 m³ on Monday and Tuesday, pauses for four days of rain and wet soil, and no pond overflows. Doing nothing would spill 114 m³ into the wadis that week.

3. What is new: our added value

In one sentence: other solutions show where the olives came from; Sonae shows that the mill didn't pollute, and as far as we know nobody else does that.

The Zero-Spill certificate

  • Every tanker of margine is recorded: where it went, how much, and that it arrived.
  • A mill whose loads were all confirmed, within the legal limits, gets a Zero-Spill certificate for a batch of oil, shown as a QR code on the bottle.
  • If even one load goes missing, the contract refuses the certificate.
  • Anyone can scan it and check the proof: no account, no middleman.

Why it matters for Tunisia: most Tunisian oil leaves in bulk and is sold under other countries' names. "Zero-Spill, made in Tunisia" gives buyers a reason to ask for Tunisian oil, and gives mills a reason to stop polluting: clean behaviour becomes something they can sell. 4. The transformation, technically

Both wastes leave the same press. This is what happens to each, with the equipment and the numbers to know. You can watch every step in 3D on the Process page. Figures are typical values to confirm with a mill and the decree.

Path A: margine becomes water and fertiliser in the soil

#StepWhat happens, technicallyNumbers
1PressingOlives are crushed into paste and spun in a 3-phase decanter (centrifuge). It separates oil, vegetation water plus washing water (margine) and solids (grignon).0.5 to 1 m³ margine and about 0.4 t grignon per tonne of olives
2Pond and lab testMargine flows to a lined pond, where solids settle and some water evaporates. A sample is tested for pH, potassium, polyphenols (the toxic part) and salinity. The result is hashed onto the Yui passport.pH 4.5 to 5.5; potassium-rich; dark and acidic
3LoadingA vacuum tanker sucks margine from the pond through a hose. Sonae only dispatches on a safe day: no rain today or tomorrow, dry soil, approved grove nearby.Tanker 15 to 18 m³; 4 trips a day
4Thin spreadingA rear spray bar (splash plate) spreads a thin, even film between the olive rows while the tanker drives. No puddles, no spraying on the trees. The farmer or the tanker's flow meter confirms the load.Max 10 m³/ha per day and 50 m³/ha per season
5In the soilSoil microbes break down the polyphenols in the top layer over a few weeks. The water stays in the root zone; potassium, phosphorus and organic matter feed the trees. Slopes, wells, wadis and rain are avoided so nothing runs off or seeps into groundwater.Olive trees need about 80 kg K₂O/ha a year
Important: margine is never put into water: not into wells, wadis or irrigation water. It fertilises the soil only. The well water comes from path B.

Path B: fitoura (grignon) becomes fuel for the well's engine

#StepWhat happens, technicallyNumbers
6DryingWet grignon is spread on a concrete drying yard and turned regularly in the sun (or dried with waste heat from the mill). Pits can be separated: they make the best fuel.From about 50% water to under 12%
7aPelletsA pellet press (ring or flat die) compresses dry grignon into small cylinders that flow evenly into the gasifier.Pellets about 6 to 8 mm
7bGasifierIn a downdraft gasifier the pellets pass through four zones: drying, pyrolysis, partial burning with little air, and reduction. Instead of flames, out comes a burnable gas (syngas: carbon monoxide, hydrogen, a little methane).2.5 kg dry grignon ≈ heat of 1 kg diesel
7cGas cleaningThe hot gas passes a cyclone (dust), a cooler and a filter that catches the tar. Olive-pomace gas is tarry, so the filter needs regular cleaning.Clean, cool gas or the engine wears fast
8Dual-fuel engine and pumpThe well pump's diesel engine takes the gas through its air intake. A small diesel injection still ignites it ("dual fuel"). The engine drives the pump, which lifts well water to drip lines in the same olive grove.About 60 to 70% less diesel
Be honest in the pitch: a small gasifier with gas cleaning costs several thousand dinars and needs maintenance, so it is a later step. Solar stays the cheapest way to pump. Today, dry grignon is mostly sold as fuel to hammams, bakeries and brick kilns, which Sonae already plans.

Why the circle works

The oil is sold. Margine returns water and potassium to the soil, so the farmer buys less fertiliser. Fitoura replaces diesel or wood, as heat for hammams and kilns or as gas for the well's engine. Well water irrigates the same trees, which bring olives back to the maâsra next winter. Every load of margine is on the Yui passport.

6. The blockchain part: Yui, the margine passport

The problem it solves: a mill is legally responsible for its margine, but nobody can check where each tanker went. Inspectors can't follow trucks, and exporters can't prove their oil was made cleanly. A public ledger that no mill, farmer or official can quietly edit fixes that, with no middleman.

1. DispatchThe mill records a load: which grove, how many m³, a hash of the lab reading. It pays Sonae's fee (0.5 TND/m³).
2. ConfirmThe farmer, or the tanker's GPS and flow meter (the "oracle"), confirms it arrived within 48 h.
3. CertifyA mill with traced loads and no lost load can certify a batch of oil, shown as a QR code on the bottle.
4. VerifyAnyone types the certificate number and sees the proof. No account needed.

Rules the contract enforces

  • Only registered mills dispatch; only the regulator (CRDA/ANPE) registers groves.
  • A load over the grove's season cap is refused, counting loads still on the road.
  • A load nobody confirms in 48 h expires: the fee goes back and the mill gets a red flag. A flagged mill can't certify oil.

Grignon market (escrow)

The mill lists grignon (demo: 30 t at 60 TND/t). A hammam pays into the contract; the money stays locked until the hammam confirms delivery, then goes to the mill. No delivery in 2 days means the buyer can take the money back.

How the AI season plan works

  1. Exact numbers first, from code, not from AI. Sonae's engine computes the volumes, tanker trips, hectares and groves needed, pond autonomy, safe spreading days (from last season's real Sfax weather), and every cost and income line in dinars.
  2. Claude Opus writes the plan around those numbers: 3 to 5 phases from mid-November to the end of February with dates, actions and the weather to expect, the main risks with how to avoid them, a dated checklist, and tips. It is told never to change a number.
  3. Sonae checks the answer: the phase shares are rescaled so the volumes always add up to the real totals.
  4. It appears live on the page with animations: headline, key figures that count up, cost and income bars, a clickable timeline of phases, risk cards, a checklist you can tick, and last season's weather. No PDF. Plans are saved, so you can reopen past ones.

A plan takes about 25 seconds and costs about 2 to 4 US cents of Opus credit. The API refuses new AI calls once the 20 USD budget is reached.

Database and security

  • Database: SQLite, built into Node (no server to install, one file in apps/api/data/, ignored by Git). The schema is plain SQL, so moving to PostgreSQL for production is easy.
  • Passwords: hashed with scrypt and a random salt; never stored or logged in clear.
  • Sessions: a random token per sign-in, valid 7 days; only its hash is stored. Suspending a user ends their sessions.
  • Roles: every action checks the role (a mill can't open admin pages, a farmer can't edit a mill).
  • Limits: sign-in and sign-up limited to 10 tries a minute; AI plans to 4 a minute; same error for wrong email or wrong password.
  • Demo accounts (admin, mill owner, farmer) are created from apps/api/.env; their passwords live only there.

11. Jury questions and how to answer

Keep answers short: one sentence, then one fact. If you don't know, say so and say how you'd find out.

The idea

QuestionAnswer
Isn't spreading margine on fields dangerous?Done badly, yes: that's the problem we solve. In thin layers, within limits, away from wells and wadis and never before rain, it returns water and potassium. Sonae enforces exactly those conditions.
Is it legal in Tunisia?Controlled spreading of margine on agricultural land is allowed under conditions. Our limits are cautious defaults, editable in Settings, and we will align them with the decree. (Check the exact text before the pitch.)
Why would a mill pay?Its pond and trucks already cost money, it risks fines, and the certificate helps it sell oil. 0.5 TND/m³ is about 750 TND a season for a medium mill.
What if a mill keeps dumping illegally?Then it gets no certificate, and inspectors see a mill with no recorded loads. Sonae makes good behaviour visible and valuable; it doesn't replace the inspector.
How is this different from existing traceability?Existing tools trace the olives' origin. We trace the mill's waste and print that proof on the bottle.
Can grignon really run a pump engine?Not directly: through a gasifier, in a dual-fuel diesel engine, saving about 60 to 70% of diesel. It costs several thousand dinars, so it's a later step; today grignon goes to hammams, bakeries and kilns.
Are your numbers real?The weather is real (Sfax, 2025-26). The replay comes from our simulation. Prices and potassium values are estimates we'll confirm with a mill.

Technology choices

QuestionAnswer
Why blockchain and not a normal database?The mill, the farmer, the regulator and the buyer don't trust each other. A database belongs to someone who can edit it; the contract's records can't be changed afterwards and anyone can verify them. We only put on chain what needs that trust (loads, caps, certificates); the plan stays off chain.
Why Ethereum / Sepolia?Sepolia is Ethereum's free test network: perfect for a demo with no real money. The same contract can move to a low-cost Ethereum network (an "L2" such as Polygon or Base) where a record costs less than a millime. Solidity and MetaMask are the most widely known tools, so auditors and developers are easy to find.
Why not Hyperledger or a private chain?A private chain is run by a few organisations, which brings back the trust problem. A public chain lets any buyer abroad check a certificate without joining anything.
Who confirms a delivery? Can't the farmer lie?The farmer or the tanker's GPS and flow meter (the "oracle") confirms. The ESP32 node we built can become that meter. A load not confirmed in 48 h expires and flags the mill.
Do farmers need crypto?No. Farmers get SMS. Wallets are for mills, the regulator and buyers; payments can stay in dinars off chain if needed.
Why a web app plus Capacitor, not native or Flutter?One React code base gives a website and an Android app at once, works offline as a PWA, and fit our 36 hours. Native or Flutter would mean two code bases.
Why run the planner on the phone, not on a server?Rural signal is weak. The planner is plain TypeScript, so it runs offline on the device and the same code runs on the server when needed.
Why Open-Meteo for weather?Free, no key, real forecasts and historical data for Tunisia, open licence (CC BY 4.0).
Why 3D?Mills, regulators and juries understand a pond, a tanker and a grove at a glance; the twin shows the plan as it plays out.
Why SMS and not an app for farmers?Many farmers have basic phones. SMS works everywhere, in Derja, with no data plan.
Did you use AI?The decisions come from a transparent, tested planner, not from AI, so every choice can be explained. Claude helped us build faster and powers optional assistant features.
Is it secure? What about privacy?Roles limit who can do what; money sits in escrow; keys are never in the code. On chain we store hashes and quantities, not personal data: farmer names and phones stay off chain.
Why SQLite and not MongoDB?Our data is relational (users, their mill or farm, packs, plans), so SQL fits better than documents. SQLite is built into Node: no server to run for a demo or a first region, and the same SQL moves to PostgreSQL when we grow.
Can I trust an AI-generated cost plan?The money and volumes are not from the AI: our engine computes them from your profile and real weather. Claude only writes the phases, risks and checklist around those numbers, and we rescale its phases to match the real totals.
How are accounts protected?Passwords hashed with scrypt, session tokens stored only as hashes, role checks on every request, and rate limits on sign-in. Admins can't be created from the sign-up form.
Is anyone paying today?No. In the demo a pack is activated without payment; a real launch adds bank transfer or D17 before activation.
Can it scale?One region (20 mills) costs about 58,000 TND to launch and breaks even around 25 mills; Tunisia has over a thousand. The software is the same for every region.
